## Pronouns (thou, thee, ye, thy, thine, mine)

The singular “thou/thee/thy/thine” and the plural “ye” all become “you/your”. Modern English no longer marks singular and plural here, so the change loses that distinction; the original keeps it. “Mine” and “thine” before a vowel sound (“mine eyes”, “thine house”) become “my/your”; at the end of a phrase “thine” becomes “yours”.

## Here, there and where (hither → here, from thence → from there)

Old English had separate words for “to here” (hither) and “from here” (hence). Today “here”, “there” and “where” do all three jobs. “Hither”, “thither”, “whither” and “whithersoever” always change. “Hence”, “thence” and “whence” change only after “from”, where the “from” is already written (“from thence” → “from there”); on their own they would need two words, so they stay, as do “hitherto” and “thitherward”.

|---|---|---|---:|
| whither | where |  | 126 |
| thither | there |  | 102 |
| thence | there | after “from” | 97 |
| hither | here |  | 74 |
| whence | where | after “from” | 37 |
| whithersoever | wherever |  | 31 |
| hence | here | after “from” | 7 |

## Single-verse exceptions

A few words are decided verse by verse. In the New Testament, “yea” becomes “yes” only where the Greek underneath is ναί (“yes”), and “nay” becomes “no” only where it is οὐ or οὐχί (“no”). Everywhere else these words mean “indeed”, “even” or “rather” (as in 1 Corinthians 2:10, “yea, the deep things of God”) and stay. In the Old Testament both stay throughout, because Hebrew has no word for “yes”. “Bare” stays “bare” where it means “naked”, and “thine” becomes “yours” in a few places where it stands alone.

## What is not changed

- **Meaning words.** Obsolete or shifted words (“let” meaning hinder, “prevent” meaning go before, “conversation” meaning conduct) are left alone. Changing them would be translation, not modernizing.
- **Word order and grammar** beyond the verb endings.
- **Names, italics (words the translators supplied), punctuation and verse divisions.**
- **The underlying text.** Every change happens on screen as the page is drawn.
